How to Make Traditional Thekua for Chhath Puja

Make the source recipe's Thekua through four stages: prepare jaggery syrup, mix a stiff dough, shape the pieces and fry them. The ingredient quantities and original method are preserved below.

Authentic Chhath Puja Thekua Recipe

Thekua is the centerpiece of Chhath Puja Prasad. This traditional treat is sweet, crunchy, and richly flavored, made using particular methods to attain the ideal texture: crispy on the outside, yet dense and crumbly within.

Note on Purity: When preparing this for the actual Chhath Prasad, be sure to use newly purchased utensils, fresh oil or ghee, and observe strict personal hygiene before entering the kitchen.

Ingredients Needed

  • 2 Cups Whole Wheat Flour (Atta) – preferably coarsely ground.
  • 3/4 Cup Pure Jaggery (Gud) - crushed.
  • 1/2 Cup Water (to melt the jaggery).
  • 3 to 4 Tablespoons Pure Ghee (for Moyan/shortening).
  • 1 Tablespoon Fennel Seeds (Saunf).
  • 2 Tablespoons Finely Chopped Dry Coconut (Nariyal).
  • Pure Ghee or Vegetable Oil for deep frying.

Step 1: Prepare the Jaggery Syrup

Place the crushed jaggery and half a cup of water in a pan. Warm gently until the jaggery fully dissolves, avoiding boiling it into a thick syrup. Strain the mixture to eliminate impurities and allow it to cool thoroughly.

Step 2: Prepare the Dough

In a large mixing bowl, place the wheat flour. Pour in the 4 tablespoons of melted ghee, known as Moyan. Using your hands, rub the ghee into the flour until the mixture looks like coarse breadcrumbs. Then, add the fennel seeds and chopped coconut.

Gradually pour in the cooled jaggery syrup and knead it into a firm, dense dough. Avoid making it soft like roti dough. The firmer the dough, the crispier the Thekua will be.

Step 3: Shape Using a Sancha

Take a small piece of dough about the size of a lemon and shape it into a smooth ball. Traditional Thekua is then pressed onto a wooden mold known as a Sancha to imprint elegant leaf or floral designs. If you lack a Sancha, flatten the ball between your palms and use a fork to create a leaf pattern.

Step 4: Deep Frying

Heat ghee or oil in a deep Kadhai over medium heat. When hot, reduce the flame. Carefully place the shaped Thekuas into the oil. Fry them patiently on a low-medium flame until they become deep golden brown. If the flame is too high, the inside will stay uncooked.

Take them off and allow to cool on a paper towel. As they cool, they will become hard and crispy. Keep in an airtight container for up to 3 weeks.
